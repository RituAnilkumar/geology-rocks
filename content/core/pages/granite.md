## What it is

Granite is a coarse-grained igneous rock made mostly of quartz and feldspar,
with smaller amounts of mica or other dark minerals. Its crystals grew slowly
as a large body of magma cooled deep underground.

## How it forms

Granite magma forms when parts of the continental crust melt, typically during
or after mountain building. The magma rises, then cools slowly over hundreds of
thousands of years, giving crystals time to grow large.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

- **Gneiss**: also coarse and light-coloured, but its minerals are arranged in
  bands. Granite has no layering.
- **Sandstone**: grains are separate and rounded, cemented together; it feels
  gritty and lacks interlocking crystals.
- **Microgranite (elvan)**: same minerals but finer-grained, often with
  scattered large crystals.

## Uses

Building and paving stone, kerbs, monuments, and — through its weathering
product kaolin — china clay.
